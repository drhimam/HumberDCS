## Section A – Review: Applying Privacy, Documentation and Consent (Questions 1–12)

---

### Question 1

A sonographer declines to discuss a patient's results with a coworker who is not involved in the patient's care. Which concept does this best illustrate?

- A) Privacy
- B) Implied consent
- C) Circle of care
- D) Confidentiality

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Confidentiality is the professional duty to protect information and share it only with authority; working in the same organization does not by itself give someone a reason to access information.

---

### Question 2

For a cardiac echo appointment, who would generally be involved in the patient's circle of care?

- A) A nurse who is not involved in the patient's care
- B) An insurer requesting results without authorization
- C) A relative waiting in the hallway, absent permission
- D) The cardiologist who ordered and will review the echo

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The cardiologist who ordered and will review the echo has a clear care purpose; being nearby does not make someone part of this patient's care.

---

### Question 3

Which documentation entry is most objective?

- A) "Patient was anxious and uncooperative."
- B) "Patient had a bad attitude."
- C) "Patient reported feeling anxious, required two additional breaks to complete the study."
- D) "Difficult study."

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** The entry records the patient's own report and the objective facts rather than labeling the person.

---

### Question 4

A sonographer says to a patient, "It'll just take a minute. Don't worry about it," without explaining what the echo involves. Which requirement of consent is most clearly missing?

- A) Informed consent
- B) Voluntary consent
- C) Written consent
- D) Substitute consent

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Without an explanation of what the examination involves, the consent is not informed — and an echo can take anywhere from thirty minutes to an hour.

---

### Question 5

- A) Finish the current view before stopping
- B) Explain why the remaining images are needed, then continue
- C) Document the concern and continue
- D) Stop scanning and attend to the patient's comfort and safety

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Consent is ongoing — scanning stops immediately and comfort and safety are addressed.

---

### Question 6

Which statement about consent forms is TRUE?

- A) A signature automatically establishes informed consent
- B) A form replaces the need for explanation
- C) A form prevents the patient from withdrawing consent
- D) A signature alone does not show that every requirement for valid consent has been met

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** A form can record a decision, but it cannot replace the information and voluntary choice needed for that decision to be valid.

---

### Question 7

A patient withdraws consent partway through an examination. What does this mean for the rest of the study?

- C) Consent must be re-established for anything further — the patient can withdraw at any time
- D) Withdrawal applies only to written consent

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Consent continues throughout the examination, and a capable patient may refuse or withdraw at any time.
