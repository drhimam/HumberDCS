## Section A – The Sonographer's Role: Scope, Competence and Authorization (Questions 1–14)

---

### Question 1

Which statement best describes the sonographer's role in acquiring and evaluating images?

- A) Acquire images only and leave all evaluation to the equipment
- B) Acquire images and make the final medical diagnosis
- C) Acquire and evaluate images, take measurements, and document observations objectively
- D) Acquire images without documenting anything if the study is normal

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** The sonographer's role includes acquiring and evaluating images, taking measurements, documenting observations, and communicating findings through the approved process.

---

### Question 2

Which pairing of terms is correct?

- A) Scope = your current knowledge and skills; Competence = the profession's legal activities
- B) Scope = your personal preferences; Competence = your years of service
- C) Scope = hospital policy only; Competence = physician approval only
- D) Scope = activities within the profession's practice; Competence = your current knowledge, skills and judgement

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Scope describes what the profession is legally permitted to do; competence describes what the individual practitioner is currently able to do.

---

### Question 3

In the scope–competence–authorization framework, what does the "authorization" question ask?

- A) Is this within the profession's legal activities?
- B) Do I have the knowledge, skills and judgement?
- C) Are the required permissions and conditions in place?
- D) Is the equipment available today?

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Authorization concerns whether the required permissions and conditions — such as a valid order and policy approval — are in place.

---

### Question 4

Which statement is TRUE?

- A) An order alone does not establish competence
- B) An order alone establishes that the sonographer is competent
- C) An order from a physician removes the need to check authorization
- D) An order replaces departmental policy

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** An order provides authorization for the examination but never establishes the practitioner's personal competence.

---

### Question 5

A trainee is asked in a teaching lab to perform a task they have not learned. What is the safest response?

- A) Try it because it belongs to the profession's scope
- B) Try it because someone asked them to
- C) Try it, then record that they were unsure
- D) Explain their limit and ask the instructor for guidance

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Recognizing a competence limit and seeking instructor guidance is the safe, professional response.

---

### Question 6

Before an examination, a sonographer should confirm all of the following EXCEPT:

- A) The correct patient
- B) The examination and the appropriate authorized order
- C) That missing or conflicting information has been clarified
- D) That the patient's family has approved the study

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Pre-examination checks cover patient identity, examination, order, and clarification of conflicts — family approval is not part of this check.

---

### Question 7

Which of the following is a legitimate reason to pause before performing a task?

- A) The task is within the profession's scope but the sonographer has not been trained in it
- B) The task has already been authorized by an order
- C) The department is busy
- D) A colleague has performed the task before

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Scope, competence, and authorization must all be satisfied; training gaps require a pause and guidance.

---

### Question 8

What should be documented when part of an examination cannot be completed?

- A) Nothing, because the missing images are obvious
- B) That the examination was complete
- C) What was obtained, what was limited, and why
- D) Nothing until another examination takes place

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Limitations and their reasons must be recorded so the record reflects what actually happened.

---

### Question 9

Which action best reflects working within competence?

- A) Performing only tasks for which training and competence have been demonstrated
- B) Attempting an unfamiliar examination to save the schedule
- C) Performing anything the profession is legally allowed to do
- D) Performing a task because a patient requested it

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The legal scope sets the outer boundary; personal competence determines what may safely be performed.

---

### Question 10

A sonographer communicates findings through:

- A) Casual conversation in the hallway
- B) Social media posts
- C) The approved reporting and communication process
- D) Verbal comments only, never documentation

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Findings must be communicated through the approved process so they reach the responsible clinician reliably.

---

### Question 11

Which sequence correctly matches the three-way check before a task?

- A) Authorization → Popularity → Convenience
- B) Competence → Speed → Billing
- C) Scope → Preference → Schedule
- D) Scope → Competence → Authorization

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The three checks are whether the activity is within the profession's legal scope, whether the practitioner is competent, and whether authorization is in place.

---

### Question 12

A student is asked to scan a patient independently because the department is short-staffed. What is the most appropriate response?

- A) Scan independently because the request came from a supervisor
- B) Explain that students practise under supervision and arrange qualified supervision
- C) Scan and document under the supervisor's name
- D) Scan if the patient agrees

**Explanation:** Students practise under supervision; staffing pressure does not change that requirement.

---

### Question 13

Which document or source defines the profession's legal activities?

- A) The sonographer's personal comfort level
- B) The busiest department's expectations
- C) The equipment's capability list
- D) The profession's scope of practice as established by legislation and standards

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Scope is defined externally by legislation and professional standards, not by personal comfort or equipment capability.

---

### Question 14

A sonographer is uncertain whether an activity is within scope, competence, and authorization. What should happen?

- A) Proceed quickly to avoid delay
- B) Pause and ask the appropriate person for guidance
