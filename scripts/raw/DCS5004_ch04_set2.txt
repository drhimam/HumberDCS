Part B – Answer Key and Explanations
1. B – Ultrasound is produced as continuous wave (CW) or pulsed wave (PW).
2. A – Pulsed ultrasound alternates transmit (on) and receive (off) periods.
3. C – On time is when the system transmits a pulse.
4. D – Off time is when the system listens for returning echoes.
5. A – CW ultrasound transmits continuously with no gaps.
6. B – Pulses give a measurable echo-return time, which allows depth to be calculated.
7. C – CW has no pulse-timing reference, so it cannot create range-resolved B-mode images.
8. A – Many scan lines together form a 2D image.
9. D – Ranging is also called echo location.
10. B – Each displayed echo left the transducer, reflected from a structure, and returned (round trip).
11. A – About 13 µs of round-trip time is needed for every centimetre of depth.
12. C – Systems assume 1540 m/s in soft tissue.
13. A – Range = (c × t) ÷ 2.
14. B – The measured time covers the trip to the reflector and back, so it is divided by two.
15. C – 26 µs ÷ 13 µs/cm = 2 cm.
16. D – 65 µs ÷ 13 = 5 cm.
17. A – 8 cm × 13 µs/cm = 104 µs.
18. B – 39 ÷ 13 = 3 cm.
19. A – The five parameters are PD, SPL, PRP, PRF and DF.
20. C – Cycles grouped with gaps between groups form pulsed ultrasound.
21. B – The gap between pulses is the off time, when echoes are received.
22. D – PD is the time for a pulse to occur (on time only).
23. A – PD is measured in microseconds.
24. B – PD represents transmit time only and does not include receive time.
25. C – PD = number of cycles × period.
26. A – A longer period means a longer PD for the same number of cycles.
27. C – More cycles per pulse means a longer PD.
28. B – T = 0.5 µs, so PD = 4 × 0.5 = 2 µs.
29. D – T = 0.2 µs, so PD = 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 µs.
30. A – 5 MHz: PD = 5 × 0.2 = 1 µs. 10 MHz: PD = 5 × 0.1 = 0.5 µs, which is half.
31. C – B-mode uses very short pulses of about 2–3 cycles.
32. B – Doppler uses longer pulses of about 5–30 cycles.
33. D – CW Doppler has no pulsing; the signal is on continuously.
34. A – PD is not directly adjustable by the sonographer.
35. C – PD depends on cycles per pulse and the period.
36. B – Period is determined by the operating frequency.
37. A – Changing frequency changes the period, so it indirectly changes PD.
38. D – Shorter PD supports better axial resolution.
39. C – A higher frequency shortens the period, so PD decreases when the cycle count is unchanged.
40. B – A shorter PD reduces echo overlap, which improves axial resolution.
41. A – SPL is the length of space occupied by one pulse.
42. C – SPL = wavelength × number of cycles.
43. D – SPL is a length, commonly in millimetres.
44. A – 4 × 0.77 mm = 3.08 mm.
45. B – 3 × 0.5 mm = 1.5 mm.
46. D – Longer wavelength means longer SPL.
47. C – Fewer cycles per pulse means shorter SPL.
48. A – A higher frequency shortens wavelength, so SPL shortens when cycles are unchanged.
49. B – SPL is not directly adjustable.
50. D – Shorter SPL improves axial resolution.
51. C – PD and SPL depend on the source (cycles, frequency, medium), not on depth.
52. A – PRP, PRF and DF change with imaging depth.
53. B – PRP is measured from the start of one pulse to the start of the next.
54. C – PRP = transmit (on) time + receive (off) time.
55. D – PRP is measured in microseconds or milliseconds.
56. A – Greater depth needs more listening time, so PRP is longer.
57. B – 10 cm × 13 µs/cm = 130 µs.
58. C – 4 × 13 = 52 µs.
59. D – PRP must be long enough for echoes from the deepest reflector to return before the next pulse.
60. A – PRP and PRF describe the timing between pulses.
61. B – Period and frequency describe a single wave (cycle). They are different from PRP and PRF.
62. C – PRF is the number of pulses transmitted each second.
63. D – PRF is measured in Hz or kHz.
64. A – Typical clinical imaging PRF is 1–10 kHz.
65. B – PRF = 1/PRP.
66. C – 1 ÷ 250 µs = 4000 Hz = 4 kHz.
67. D – 1 ÷ 5000 Hz = 0.0002 s = 200 µs.
68. A – PRF is inversely related to imaging depth.
69. B – Shallower depth means shorter PRP and higher PRF.
70. C – 1 ÷ 130 µs ≈ 7692 Hz ≈ 7.7 kHz.
71. D – 1 ÷ 65 µs ≈ 15,385 Hz ≈ 15.4 kHz.
72. A – PRF is determined by propagation velocity and imaging depth.
73. B – One pulse produces one scan line of image information.
74. C – The shallowest useful depth maximises PRF, which improves frame rate and temporal resolution.
75. D – Higher PRF supports a higher frame rate and better temporal resolution.
76. A – Duty factor is the percentage of time the system is transmitting.
77. B – DF = (PD / PRP) × 100.
78. C – DF is expressed as a percentage.
79. D – 2 ÷ 200 = 0.01, which is 1%.
80. A – 1 ÷ 250 = 0.004, which is 0.4%.
81. B – PD and DF move in the same direction.
82. C – PRP and DF move in opposite directions.
83. D – Greater depth means longer PRP and therefore lower DF.
84. A – Shallower depth means shorter PRP and therefore higher DF.
85. B – CW is always transmitting, so DF = 100%.
86. C – PD is not adjusted directly, so DF changes indirectly through depth (PRP).
87. A – Depth doubles, so PRP doubles and PRF is halved.
88. C – Depth doubles, so PRP doubles; with PD constant, DF is halved.
89. B – Frame rate is the number of frames per second.
90. C – Frame time is the time needed to create one complete frame.
91. D – Frame rate = 1 / frame time.
92. A – 1 ÷ 0.050 s = 20 Hz.
93. D – 1 ÷ 40 Hz = 0.025 s = 25 ms.
94. B – Greater depth lowers PRF, so frames take longer to build and frame rate falls.
95. A – Scanned modalities sweep the beam across a region to build an image.
96. C – B-mode is a scanned modality. M-mode, A-mode and CW Doppler are non-scanned.
97. B – Non-scanned modalities transmit the beam in the same direction repeatedly.
98. C – M-mode is non-scanned (like A-mode, CW Doppler and single-gate PW Doppler).
99. D – Non-scanned modalities present greater thermal risks than scanned modalities.
100. A – Colour Doppler sends multiple acoustic lines per colour display line, called a colour packet.
