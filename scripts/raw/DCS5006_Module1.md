## Section A – The Profession and the Sonographer's Role (Questions 1–14)

---

### Question 1

Which statement best describes the profession of diagnostic medical sonography?

- A) The use of ionizing radiation to produce cross-sectional images of the body
- B) The use of magnetic fields and radiofrequency pulses to evaluate tissue
- C) The use of high-frequency sound waves to produce images and information used to assess patients
- D) The administration of radiopharmaceuticals to evaluate organ function

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Sonography uses high-frequency sound waves to generate images and patient data. The other options describe radiography, magnetic resonance imaging, and nuclear medicine.

---

### Question 2

Which component is part of the Ontario scope of practice for a diagnostic medical sonographer?

- A) Assessing the patient before, during, and after the examination
- B) Diagnosing disease from the images obtained
- C) Prescribing the imaging examination based on the sonographer's findings
- D) Independently initiating examinations without any order

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The Ontario scope includes using sound waves for diagnostic ultrasound, evaluating the images and resulting patient data, and assessing the patient before, during, and after the exam. Diagnosis and ordering remain the responsibility of the ordering clinician.

---

### Question 3

A student observing in an ultrasound department is asked by a patient, "Are you a doctor?" What is the most appropriate response?

- A) "I am a student sonographer working under supervision."
- B) "Yes, I am a doctor in training."
- C) "I am part of the medical team, so yes."
- D) "I cannot answer that question."

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Students must identify themselves as students and describe their role accurately. Misrepresenting one's status to a patient is dishonest and undermines informed consent.

---

## Section B – Scope of Practice Versus Individual Competence (Questions 15–32)

---

### Question 15

Which statement correctly describes the relationship between scope of practice and individual competence?

- A) Scope of practice and competence mean the same thing
- B) Competence is set by legislation; scope is set by the individual
- C) If a task is within scope, every registrant is automatically competent to perform it
- D) Scope is what you are legally allowed to do; competence is what you are actually trained and able to do

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Scope defines the legal and professional boundaries of practice, while competence reflects the individual's actual knowledge, skills, and judgement.

---

### Question 16

A task may be within a sonographer's scope of practice, but the sonographer has never been trained to perform it. What should happen?

- A) The sonographer should attempt it and learn from any mistakes
- B) The sonographer should ask a colleague to supervise informally without documentation
- C) The sonographer should not perform it until appropriately trained and assessed as competent
- D) The sonographer should perform it because the order authorizes it

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Being within scope does not establish individual competence. The sonographer must obtain the required training and competency assessment first.

---

### Question 17

- D) Is it legally within my scope? Am I trained and competent? Is it permitted by the order, standards, and policy?

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The three pre-procedure questions address legality, personal competence, and authorization. All three must be satisfied before proceeding.

---
